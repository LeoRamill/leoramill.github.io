> *At the beginning you feel so full of life and it's… exciting, but time makes everything predictable.. it's like he doesn't see me anymore.. like he replaced me with someone else… I've lost the parts that define who I am or who I could be… and I'm afraid I'll never get them back… I shouldn't be telling you these things*
>

Time… Time… is it possible that time makes everything disappear? That it makes the passion disappear? But above all, does it make a person ordinary, does it make someone **taken for granted**?

Maybe that's why a relationship ends in the end, whether it's friendship, family or love. Maybe that's why it makes the loss more painful… you have all the love in the world, except it has been taken for granted, except nobody has fought for it in a long time.

And the cruel part is that nothing happens. There's no betrayal, no door slammed at three in the morning. There are only days that look like each other, and one evening you realise you haven't asked a real question in months. Love doesn't end with a bang: it ends by repetition.

What hurts most in what she says isn't *I don't love you*. It's *it's like he doesn't see me anymore*. Because that's the whole thing: being seen. When you stop looking at someone, you quietly swap them for the idea you already have of them, and you keep talking to that idea every day, convinced it's still them. Nobody needs to knock at your door and offer to build you a copy of the person you live with: you have already built one yourself.

So who is the foe? Not the person in front of you. Maybe it's time. Maybe it's the habit that has stopped being care: the same gestures, emptied out, repeated because they cost nothing.

Love in the end isn't only attraction: attraction is the epilogue of the journey, the part that's already written, the part that happens to you without any effort. Love is the chapters, and the chapters are the ones you have to write, one day at a time. Love is knowing how to tend our own garden, it is, as we can see in the film, using the rain to make our small plants grow. It's not by chance that the lands shown around them are bare by now; only one tree is still intact: the tree of the one who kept giving love, day after day. Where water is scarce, whatever you don't water dies. And nothing dies from a single dry day: it dies from many.

But what is the limit for walking away from this non-love, from this foe? The limit is probably when you no longer feel like yourself, when you no longer recognise your own identity: when your passions have been destroyed by the other, who has gone from companion to enemy. And there you need a plane to escape on, you need the courage to leave.

Leaving isn't revenge, and it isn't a defeat either. It's the last act of respect for the person you could still become. And nobody lands that plane in your field for you: you're the one who has to call it.

And what if you're the other person instead? The one who destroyed that love. Well… then it's staying in the solitude, turning it over, suffering your mistakes and working through them; only that way can you avoid making the same mistake when destiny gives you another chance to love, another book whose epilogue has already been drafted, but whose chapters are yours to write.

There's no shortcut there. Nobody can forgive you into becoming someone different; you have to sit in it until you've understood what you stopped looking at. The point isn't the guilt: it's the attention. Learning to look again.

So yes, let time take its course. Just don't let it do the work in your place: time writes the epilogue, the chapters are yours.
